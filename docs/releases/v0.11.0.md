## Mnemosyne 0.11.0

### Live speaker labels you can trust
On a computer with an NVIDIA graphics card, the live transcript now tells speakers apart with NVIDIA's Nemotron model in streaming mode, the same model that labels the final transcript. The live view no longer guesses from voice fingerprints and fixes itself every 30 seconds:
- **The right speaker as the line appears.** On four recorded test meetings (AMI), 96–97% of live words appear with the right speaker, against 92–96% before, and they no longer wait up to 30 seconds for a correction.
- **One label per person.** A speaker keeps one label for the whole recording, changing only to their name once their voice is recognised. Before, one person could show up under several labels (12 or 13 labels for 4 people was common).
- **Split at the change of speaker.** When someone takes over mid-sentence, the line is split where they start talking.
- **Named, and the same across parts.** Once someone has said a few seconds, a saved voice gives them their name. If you stop and record into the same meeting again, the people from the first part keep their labels.
- **Light on the GPU.** About 3% of the graphics card per recorded channel, and around 600 MB of its memory.

It tracks up to eight voices on the system-audio channel (your own mic is separate); in bigger meetings some live labels get merged, and the final transcript after you stop is unaffected. Without an NVIDIA card nothing changes. Settings → Transcription → Live speaker labels chooses Nemotron streaming or the previous voice clustering (Automatic picks Nemotron when it can).

### Install
The app offers this update itself if you run the AppImage, deb or rpm. Otherwise download the AppImage (`chmod +x`, then run), the offline AppImage, the `.deb`, the `.rpm` or the `.flatpak` (`flatpak install --user <file>`). Verify downloads with `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`.
