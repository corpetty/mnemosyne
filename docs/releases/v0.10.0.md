## Mnemosyne 0.10.0

### Remote access
- **Reach your meetings from another computer, anywhere.** Turn on remote access on the computer that records (Settings → Server mode), choose "Pair a computer" and paste the one-time invite into the other computer's Settings → Connection. The two connect end to end encrypted over iroh (directly when they can, through a relay otherwise), with nothing to open on your router; only paired computers get in, and removing one takes effect at once.
- **Local first.** At home, the two find each other on the local network and connect directly, never through a relay; relays are only the fallback away from home.
- **Your own relays, if you like.** A relay setting points remote access at relays you run; `deploy/relay/` has a ready-to-run iroh-relay setup, and docs/remote-access.md covers it and Tailscale.
- **Phones pair with a one-time code.** The phone QR code no longer carries the server's API token: Settings shows a code that works once, for 10 minutes, and the phone gets its own token that can only upload recordings and can be removed on its own.

### During the meeting
- **Bookmarks.** Mark a moment as important while recording (the Mark button, Ctrl+M, "Mark this moment" in the tray, or `mnemosyne --mark` for a desktop shortcut), or later on a transcript line. Bookmarks sit above the transcript, the summary makes sure to cover them, and they go into the Obsidian note.
- **An agenda the copilot follows.** Write the points a meeting should get through, or let them come from the calendar event's description. While recording, the copilot ticks them off as they come up; the summary lists the ones never discussed.
- **Resources.** Links and files for a meeting (the deck, the doc, the ticket), in a library shared by every meeting, so what was shared last week is at hand this week. Links in the calendar invite are attached on their own (not the call's join link). The summary reads them for context and names; the Obsidian note links them and copies files into the vault.

### Less setup
- **Meeting types.** A standup, a 1:1, a customer call: pick words from the title, and the type chooses the summary style, adds instructions, picks the Obsidian folder, keeps the meeting local-only, or records it when it starts on the calendar. Each meeting's header has a type picker.
- **The glossary learns from your corrections.** Fix a misheard name in a transcript and Mnemosyne offers to remember it, fixing the rest of that meeting and every one after.
- **Notes from other assistants.** Paste or upload Gemini's notes from Google Meet, Zoom's meeting summary, Otter or Teams Copilot notes. The summary fills gaps from them (the transcript wins); a meeting you didn't record can be summarized from them alone.

### Everyday
- **Obsidian daily note.** Each exported meeting adds a line and your open tasks from it to that day's daily note, found the way Obsidian's Daily Notes plugin finds it.
- **The GPU is freed when idle.** The speech models unload after 15 idle minutes (Settings → Transcription), giving games and other GPU work their memory back.
- **Editing long transcripts is fast again.** Editing, merging or splitting a line of a long meeting could take over a minute in a large library; it now takes a fraction of a second (the search index is rebuilt once, at the first start).
- **A new icon**, and a website: https://corpetty.github.io/mnemosyne/

### Install
The app offers this update itself if you run the AppImage, deb or rpm. Otherwise download the AppImage (`chmod +x`, then run), the offline AppImage, the `.deb`, the `.rpm` or the `.flatpak` (`flatpak install --user <file>`). Verify downloads with `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS`.
